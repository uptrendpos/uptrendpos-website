The problems

Common problems artificial jewellery shop owners face without POS software

An artificial jewellery shop sells hundreds of small, low-price pieces. One jhumka comes in many designs and colours, girls pick five things at once, and the Eid and wedding rush brings a crowd to the counter. A copy and a calculator cannot keep up. Good artificial jewellery shop POS software fixes these problems first.

  • The problem

    Which colour is in stock?

    The same earring comes in gold tone, silver tone and red stone. A customer asks for the red one and your salesman opens every box to check.

    Every design in every colour is its own item with its own stock. You see it on the screen in a second.

  • The problem

    Prices from memory

    Small pieces have no price written on them. Each salesman tells a different price, and you lose money on the busy days.

    Each item has its own price in the system, and barcode labels with design and price on Enterprise and Custom.

  • The problem

    Slow bills in the rush

    On Chand Raat ten girls wait at the counter. Every bill is added on a calculator and written by hand, and the line gets longer.

    Scan the small tag or search by design or code, and the item is on the bill. Hold a bill and serve the next customer.

  • The problem

    Udhaar on loose slips

    A regular customer takes a bridal set and pays part now. The rest is on a slip, and the slip is lost by next month.

    Udhaar on the customer ledger with every payment, and a statement on WhatsApp in one tap.

  • The problem

    Stock goes missing

    Small pieces are easy to lose, break or give as a sample. At the end of the month the stock never matches the copy.

    Damage, sample and adjustment entries, plus a stock audit, so you know where every piece went.

  • The problem

    Load-shedding stops sales

    The light goes on the busiest evening of the year. The computer is off and customers walk out.

    Cash bills keep running on Windows and Android without internet and sync later.
