The problems

Common problems battery shop owners face without POS software

A battery shop sells heavy, costly items in many sizes. Mechanics buy on udhaar, families buy UPS batteries in the load-shedding rush, and every customer asks about warranty. A register and a calculator cannot keep all of this straight. Good battery shop POS software fixes these problems first.

  • The problem

    Which ampere is in stock?

    A customer asks for a 50 ampere dry battery. Your boy walks to the godown, counts, and comes back to say "khatam ho gayi".

    Every battery in every ampere and type is its own item with its own stock. You see it on the screen in a second.

  • The problem

    Warranty on a loose slip

    You write "12 months" on a slip. The customer loses it, then comes back after eight months and argues about the date.

    Warranty notes on the sale, printed on the bill or sent on WhatsApp as a picture or PDF, so the promise is on record.

  • The problem

    Mechanics' udhaar in a khata

    Workshops and electricians take batteries on udhaar every week. The khata fills with cuttings and nobody agrees on the balance.

    Udhaar on the customer ledger with every payment, and a statement on WhatsApp in one tap.

  • The problem

    UPS sets sold on qist

    In summer, families buy two tubular batteries for the UPS and pay monthly. The register never matches what was collected.

    Qist plans with a guarantor on Enterprise and Custom, with each installment on the record.

  • The problem

    Dead battery from the supplier

    A new battery comes in dead. It sits in a corner, the supplier never gives credit, and your stock count is wrong.

    Record it as damage or return it to the supplier, so stock and the supplier balance stay right.

  • The problem

    Load-shedding stops the counter

    The light goes, the internet goes, and the shop is full of people who need a UPS battery right now.

    Our battery shop POS software keeps cash bills running on the Windows and Android apps and syncs when the internet is back.

Yes. Write the warranty note on the sale, for example "12 months". It shows on the thermal receipt or A4 invoice, and on the bill you send on WhatsApp as a picture or PDF.

Not for battery shops today. Warranty notes are written on the sale, and you find the bill on the customer's ledger by name or phone number.
